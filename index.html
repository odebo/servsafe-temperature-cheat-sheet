ServSafe Manager Temperature Cheat Sheet: Every Number You Must Memorize

Temperature questions are the single largest cluster on the ServSafe Manager exam. These are the numbers you need cold, and the way the exam likes to disguise each one.

The core table

What you are doingNumberHow the exam hides it
Cold holding41°F or belowGives you a thermometer reading of 44°F and asks for the corrective action, not the rule
Hot holding135°F or aboveBuries it in a buffet scenario with a time component
Cooling, first stage135°F to 70°F in 2 hoursAsks which stage was missed
Cooling, second stage70°F to 41°F in 4 more hoursOffers “6 hours” as a distractor with no checkpoint
Reheating for hot holding165°F for 15 secondsConfuses it with the 135°F hot-holding number
Receiving frozen food0°F or belowPairs it with a rejected-delivery question
Receiving refrigerated food41°F or belowAdds a signed-invoice distractor
Cooking poultry165°F for 15 secondsMixes it with ground meat at 155°F
Cooking ground meat155°F for 17 secondsOffers 165°F as the “safe answer” trap
Cooking whole cuts of beef or pork145°F for 15 secondsAdds a rest-time component
The two most common traps: reheating (165°F, not 135°F) and the two-stage cooling checkpoint (2 hours to 70°F, then 4 more to 41°F).

How to actually memorize these

  1. Group by verb, not by number: holding, cooling, reheating, receiving, cooking.
  2. Learn the four exceptions, not the 90 percent: ground meat, poultry, whole cuts, and leftovers.
  3. Re-derive instead of recall. If you know the danger zone is 41°F to 135°F, most of the table follows.
